📜 Ebionites: The Early Followers

Q: Who were the Ebionites?

A: An early Jewish-Christian sect of the first century who followed Jesus and observed the Mosaic law. They were labelled heretics for their beliefs, although they were the closest to the time of Jesus and are the earliest followers of Jesus. They followed the below beliefs and practices:

They believed Jesus was the human, divinely chosen Messiah who followed the Jewish law, but was not God.

They followed Jewish customs like circumcision and a kosher diet, and they rejected animal sacrifices.

The Ebionites considered Paul and his Gentile followers to be apostates, believing he strayed from Jesus' original Jewish teachings.

The Ebionites' primary source was the Hebrew or Aramaic Bible, and they used a version of the Gospel of Matthew, possibly a Hebrew or Aramaic one, and their own Gospel of the Ebionites.

Their movement likely arose after the destruction of the Jewish Temple in 70 CE, with members possibly settling in Transjordan and Syria to avoid persecution. As mainstream Christianity gained dominance and established its own doctrines, the Ebionites, with their distinct views, eventually faded by the fourth or fifth century CE.

❗ Heresies

These are some of the popular heresies, there are many more not mentioned.

Sabellianism: Father, son, and holy spirit are not 3 distinct persons, rather different manifestations of god.

Marcionism: Old testament god is not the same as the new testament god.

Monarchianism: The father alone is god, and that the son and holy spirit are separate, non-divine beings. They were also known as unitarians.

Modalism: Father, son, and holy spirit are different modes of god.

Arianism: Jesus is not fully god rather a created being and not begotten.

Binatarianism: Godhead only includes father and son, the holy spirit is not a separate person rather an aspect of them.

Subordinationism: The son and the holy spirit are not coequal with the father, rather subordinate to the father in nature, role, or both.

Macedonianism: Denying the godhood of the holy spirit, also known as Pneumatomachianism.

Apollonarianism: Jesus had a human body and sensitive soul but a divine mind (the Logos) instead of a human rational mind.

Monophysitism: Also known as Eutychianism, Jesus had only one divine nature, not two.

Nestorianism: Jesus was two separate persons, one divine and one human.
