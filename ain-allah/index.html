The Qur’an

The verses of highness, istiwa, ascent and the names of Allah. All seven passages mentioning istiwa over the Throne are included.

الْقُرْآنُ الْكَرِيمُ

The Most Merciful, above the Throne

Qur’an

ٱلرَّحْمَـٰنُ عَلَى ٱلْعَرْشِ ٱسْتَوَىٰ

English meaning

The Most Merciful rose over the Throne.

Qur’an 20:5
Context & attribution

Istiwa over the Throne is mentioned in seven passages. In the Athari explanation, it is affirmed as befits Allah, without describing how or comparing Him with His creation. The six companion passages below are excerpts; each link opens the complete verse.

The One in heaven

Qur’an

ءَأَمِنتُم مَّن فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ أَن يَخْسِفَ بِكُمُ ٱلْأَرْضَ فَإِذَا هِىَ تَمُورُ

English meaning

Qur’an 67:16
Context & attribution

The wording is man fi al-samaʾ. In the Athari explanation, this affirms Allah’s highness; it does not mean that the created heavens surround or contain Him.

Good words ascend to Him

Qur’an

إِلَيْهِ يَصْعَدُ ٱلْكَلِمُ ٱلطَّيِّبُ وَٱلْعَمَلُ ٱلصَّـٰلِحُ يَرْفَعُهُۥ

Excerpt · English meaning

To Him the good word ascends, and righteous work raises it.

Qur’an 35:10
Context & attribution

This excerpt directly states that good words ascend to Him. The relationship of “righteous work” to “raises it” has more than one explanation in tafsir; the quoted evidence here is the preceding clause of ascent.

The angels and the Spirit ascend to Him in a day whose measure is fifty thousand years.

Qur’an 70:4

Above His servants

Qur’an

وَهُوَ ٱلْقَاهِرُ فَوْقَ عِبَادِهِۦ ۚ وَهُوَ ٱلْحَكِيمُ ٱلْخَبِيرُ

English meaning

He is the Subduer above His servants, and He is the Wise, the Fully Aware.

Qur’an 6:18
Context & attribution

This affirms His supremacy over His servants. Athari scholars read this with the more explicit texts of highness and istiwa.

Qur’an

English meaning

To Him belongs everything in the heavens and everything on earth, and He is the Most High, the Magnificent.

Pharaoh’s challenge concerning the God of Musa

Qur’an

وَقَالَ فِرْعَوْنُ يَـٰهَـٰمَـٰنُ ٱبْنِ لِى صَرْحًا لَّعَلِّىٓ أَبْلُغُ ٱلْأَسْبَـٰبَ

English meaning

Qur’an 40:36

These are Pharaoh’s words, quoted by the Qur’an in condemnation of his arrogance. Athari authors cite his attempted ascent in their discussion of Musa’s message; this is contextual evidence, not an endorsement of Pharaoh’s reasoning.

أَسْبَـٰبَ ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ فَأَطَّلِعَ إِلَىٰٓ إِلَـٰهِ مُوسَىٰ وَإِنِّى لَأَظُنُّهُۥ كَـٰذِبًا ۚ وَكَذَٰلِكَ زُيِّنَ لِفِرْعَوْنَ سُوٓءُ عَمَلِهِۦ وَصُدَّ عَنِ ٱلسَّبِيلِ ۚ وَمَا كَيْدُ فِرْعَوْنَ إِلَّا فِى تَبَابٍ

English meaning

The authentic Sunnah

Reports from Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, with the speaker, wording and context kept clear.

السُّنَّةُ الصَّحِيحَةُ

The question put to the believing slave woman

Ṣaḥīḥ

Narrated by Muʿāwiyah ibn al-Ḥakam al-Sulamī

فَقَالَ لَهَا «أَيْنَ اللَّهُ». قَالَتْ فِي السَّمَاءِ. قَالَ «مَنْ أَنَا». قَالَتْ أَنْتَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ. قَالَ «أَعْتِقْهَا فَإِنَّهَا مُؤْمِنَةٌ».

Excerpt · English meaning

He asked her, ‘Where is Allah?’ She replied, ‘In heaven.’ He asked, ‘Who am I?’ She replied, ‘You are Allah’s Messenger.’ He said, ‘Set her free, for she is a believer.’

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 537a
Context & attribution

An excerpt from the report of Muʿawiyah. The Prophet treated his striking of the enslaved shepherdess as grave, before asking her these questions. The answer is fi al-samaʾ: Athari scholars understand it as highness, without containment in the created heavens.

Nothing is above You

Ṣaḥīḥ

Narrated by Abū Hurayrah; transmitted through Abū Ṣāliḥ and Suhayl

وَأَنْتَ الظَّاهِرُ فَلَيْسَ فَوْقَكَ شَىْءٌ

Excerpt · English meaning

You are al-Ẓāhir, and there is nothing above You.

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2713a
Context & attribution

An excerpt from the supplication before sleep. The divine name al-Zahir is retained so that the Prophet’s explanation, “nothing is above You”, remains visible. The full supplication also mentions al-Awwal, al-Akhir and al-Batin.

The trusted messenger of the One in heaven

Ṣaḥīḥ

Narrated by Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī

أَلاَ تَأْمَنُونِي وَأَنَا أَمِينُ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاءِ، يَأْتِينِي خَبَرُ السَّمَاءِ صَبَاحًا وَمَسَاءً

Excerpt · English meaning

Will you not trust me, when I am trusted by the One in heaven, and news from heaven comes to me morning and evening?

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4351
Context & attribution

Spoken after the Prophet’s distribution of gold was questioned. Athari scholars cite man fi al-samaʾ alongside the other texts of highness, without taking the created heaven to enclose Allah.

The written book above the Throne

Ṣaḥīḥ

Narrated by Abū Hurayrah

لَمَّا قَضَى اللَّهُ الْخَلْقَ كَتَبَ فِي كِتَابِهِ، فَهْوَ عِنْدَهُ فَوْقَ الْعَرْشِ إِنَّ رَحْمَتِي غَلَبَتْ غَضَبِي

Excerpt · English meaning

When Allah completed creation, He wrote in His book—which is with Him above the Throne—‘My mercy has prevailed over My anger.’

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 3194
Context & attribution

“It is with Him above the Throne” refers grammatically to the written book. Athari scholars cite “with Him above the Throne” as part of the cumulative evidence. The declaration of Allah’s mercy is preserved with the quotation.

The descent in the last third of the night

Ṣaḥīḥ

Narrated by Abū Hurayrah

يَنْزِلُ رَبُّنَا تَبَارَكَ وَتَعَالَى كُلَّ لَيْلَةٍ إِلَى السَّمَاءِ الدُّنْيَا حِينَ يَبْقَى ثُلُثُ اللَّيْلِ الآخِرُ يَقُولُ مَنْ يَدْعُونِي فَأَسْتَجِيبَ لَهُ مَنْ يَسْأَلُنِي فَأُعْطِيَهُ مَنْ يَسْتَغْفِرُنِي فَأَغْفِرَ لَهُ

Excerpt · English meaning

Our Lord, blessed and exalted, descends each night to the lowest heaven when the final third of the night remains. He says, ‘Who calls upon Me, so that I may answer him? Who asks of Me, so that I may give to him? Who seeks My forgiveness, so that I may forgive him?’

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 1145
Context & attribution

The Athari approach affirms this descent as befits Allah, without specifying its modality or likening it to created movement. The report also encourages supplication, asking and repentance in the final third of the night.

The angels ascend after attending the prayers

Ṣaḥīḥ

Narrated by Abū Hurayrah

يَتَعَاقَبُونَ فِيكُمْ مَلاَئِكَةٌ بِاللَّيْلِ وَمَلاَئِكَةٌ بِالنَّهَارِ، وَيَجْتَمِعُونَ فِي صَلاَةِ الْفَجْرِ وَصَلاَةِ الْعَصْرِ، ثُمَّ يَعْرُجُ الَّذِينَ بَاتُوا فِيكُمْ، فَيَسْأَلُهُمْ وَهْوَ أَعْلَمُ بِهِمْ كَيْفَ تَرَكْتُمْ عِبَادِي فَيَقُولُونَ تَرَكْنَاهُمْ وَهُمْ يُصَلُّونَ، وَأَتَيْنَاهُمْ وَهُمْ يُصَلُّونَ

Excerpt · English meaning

Angels take turns among you by night and by day, meeting at the dawn and afternoon prayers. Then those who spent the night among you ascend. He asks them, although He knows them better, ‘How did you leave My servants?’ They say, ‘We left them praying, and we came to them while they were praying.’

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 555
Context & attribution

This wording states that the angels ascend and that Allah questions them; it does not add “to Him” after “ascend”. It is supporting evidence alongside more explicit texts, and it affirms that Allah already knows His servants’ condition.

The upward gesture at ʿArafah

Ṣaḥīḥ

Narrated by Jābir ibn ʿAbd Allāh; through Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī and Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad

قَالُوا نَشْهَدُ أَنَّكَ قَدْ بَلَّغْتَ وَأَدَّيْتَ وَنَصَحْتَ. فَقَالَ بِإِصْبَعِهِ السَّبَّابَةِ يَرْفَعُهَا إِلَى السَّمَاءِ وَيَنْكُتُهَا إِلَى النَّاسِ «اللَّهُمَّ اشْهَدِ اللَّهُمَّ اشْهَدْ». ثَلاَثَ مَرَّاتٍ

Excerpt · English meaning

They said, ‘We testify that you have conveyed the message, fulfilled your duty and given sincere counsel.’ He then raised his index finger towards heaven and directed it towards the people, saying, ‘O Allah, bear witness; O Allah, bear witness,’ three times.

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 1218a
Context & attribution

From Jabir’s account of the Farewell Pilgrimage, during the sermon at ʿArafah. The upward gesture is the narrator’s description of the Prophet’s action; “O Allah, bear witness” is his speech.

Zaynab’s words: above seven heavens

Ṣaḥīḥ · Companion report

Narrated by Anas ibn Mālik, reporting the words of Zaynab bint Jaḥsh

زَوَّجَكُنَّ أَهَالِيكُنَّ، وَزَوَّجَنِي اللَّهُ تَعَالَى مِنْ فَوْقِ سَبْعِ سَمَوَاتٍ

These are Zaynab bint Jahsh’s words, reported by Anas, concerning her marriage mentioned in Qur’an 33:37. They are evidence from a Companion, not a direct saying of the Prophet.

The ascent and return to his Lord

Ṣaḥīḥ

Narrated by Anas ibn Mālik

ثُمَّ عَرَجَ بِنَا إِلَى السَّمَاءِ السَّابِعَةِ … فَأَوْحَى اللَّهُ إِلَىَّ مَا أَوْحَى فَفَرَضَ عَلَىَّ خَمْسِينَ صَلاَةً فِي كُلِّ يَوْمٍ وَلَيْلَةٍ فَنَزَلْتُ إِلَى مُوسَى … قَالَ فَرَجَعْتُ إِلَى رَبِّي فَقُلْتُ يَا رَبِّ خَفِّفْ عَلَى أُمَّتِي

Excerpt · English meaning

Then he took us up to the seventh heaven … Allah revealed to me what He revealed and required of me fifty prayers in every day and night. Then I descended to Moses … He said, ‘I returned to my Lord and said: My Lord, ease the burden upon my community.’

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 162a
Context & attribution

Ellipses mark separate excerpts from the same narration. Jibril is the one who takes the Prophet upwards. The fuller account includes Ibrahim, the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary and Musa’s advice to seek a reduction in the prayers.

The Throne above al-Firdaws

Ṣaḥīḥ

Narrated by Abū Hurayrah

فَإِذَا سَأَلْتُمُ اللَّهَ فَسَلُوهُ الْفِرْدَوْسَ، فَإِنَّهُ أَوْسَطُ الْجَنَّةِ وَأَعْلَى الْجَنَّةِ، وَفَوْقَهُ عَرْشُ الرَّحْمَنِ، وَمِنْهُ تَفَجَّرُ أَنْهَارُ الْجَنَّةِ

Excerpt · English meaning

When you ask Allah, ask Him for al-Firdaws: it is the central and highest part of Paradise. Above it is the Throne of the Most Merciful, and from it the rivers of Paradise spring forth.

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 7423
Context & attribution

This text places the Throne above al-Firdaws and is read alongside the texts concerning istiwa. Awsat is translated here as “central”; it is also explained as “best”.

Deeds are raised to Him

Ṣaḥīḥ

Narrated by Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī

يُرْفَعُ إِلَيْهِ عَمَلُ اللَّيْلِ قَبْلَ عَمَلِ النَّهَارِ وَعَمَلُ النَّهَارِ قَبْلَ عَمَلِ اللَّيْلِ

Excerpt · English meaning

The deeds of the night are raised to Him before the deeds of the day, and the deeds of the day before the deeds of the night.

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 179a
Context & attribution

An excerpt from the report describing Allah’s freedom from sleep. Here the wording explicitly says that deeds are “raised to Him”. No distance or modality is described.

Hands stretched towards heaven in supplication

Ṣaḥīḥ

Narrated by Abū Hurayrah

Excerpt · English meaning

Then he mentioned a man on a long journey, dishevelled and dusty, stretching both hands towards heaven: ‘My Lord! My Lord!’ Yet his food is unlawful, his drink is unlawful, his clothing is unlawful, and he has been nourished by what is unlawful. How, then, could his prayer be answered?

Context & attribution

Supporting context: the main teaching concerns lawful sustenance and answered supplication. The passage describes hands extended towards heaven in duʿa; that gesture alone is not an independent verbal definition of Allah’s highness.

Why we say ‘above’

The word fawq in revelation, the choice of English wording, and affirmation without likening Allah to His creation.

فَوْقَ الْعَرْشِ

Fawq: “above” is wording from revelation

Qur’an & Sunnah

The word فَوْقَ (fawqa, “above”) occurs in the revealed texts themselves. In this Athari reading, affirming Allah’s highness follows these texts, without likening Him to His creation.

“They fear their Lord above them”

يَخَافُونَ رَبَّهُم مِّن فَوْقِهِمْ وَيَفْعَلُونَ مَا يُؤْمَرُونَ ۩

English meaning

They fear their Lord above them and carry out what they are commanded.

Qur’an 16:50

The wording in An-Nahl is مِنْ فَوْقِهِمْ (min fawqihim). After min, the word is pronounced fawqi, with a kasrah. In the next verse below, it is fawqa, with a fatḥah.

“Above His servants”

وَهُوَ ٱلْقَاهِرُ فَوْقَ عِبَادِهِۦ ۚ وَهُوَ ٱلْحَكِيمُ ٱلْخَبِيرُ

English meaning

He is the Subduer above His servants, and He is the Wise, the Fully Aware.

Qur’an 6:18

“There is nothing above You”

وَأَنْتَ الظَّاهِرُ فَلَيْسَ فَوْقَكَ شَىْءٌ

Excerpt · English meaning

You are al-Ẓāhir, and there is nothing above You.

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2713a

The Qur’an also uses fawqa in Al-Anʿam 6:61. These passages establish the wording “above”; the verses specifically concerning the Throne use istiwāʾ ʿalā al-ʿarsh, as in Ta-Ha 20:5 and the other six passages.

Why “above the Throne” is clearer English

Translation note

This page prefers “above the Throne” when stating the belief, and “rose over the Throne” when translating istiwāʾ. “Above” expresses highness directly, without adding an image of a body sitting on a surface.

In ordinary English, “on” often suggests contact or support, as when something rests on a table. “Above” is therefore a clearer choice for readers who might otherwise imagine that kind of relationship. This is a reason for careful translation, not a claim that every use of the English word “on” necessarily asserts physical contact.

Cambridge Dictionary: the spatial sense of “on”

The Arabic of revelation remains عَلَى الْعَرْشِ اسْتَوَىٰ. We affirm that wording as well as the texts using fawq. An English preference does not replace the Qur’an’s ʿalā, nor establish a theory about contact, distance or the manner of Allah’s istiwa.

The expression “above His Throne” is also transmitted from the early scholars:

Al-Awzaʿī: above His Throne

كنا والتابعون متوافرون نقول: إن الله تعالى فوق عرشه، ونؤمن بما وردت به السنة من صفاته.

Excerpt · English meaning

While the Successors were still numerous, we used to say: Allah Most High is above His Throne, and we believe in the attributes reported in the Sunnah.

Al-Dhahabī, Siyar aʿlām al-nubalāʾ, 7:121, biography of al-Awzaʿī; transmitted through al-Bayhaqī's al-Asmāʾ wa-l-ṣifāt.

Al-Dhahabī cautions against adding assertions about contact or its absence, treating those as questions about the unknown manner, while affirming in general that Allah is above His Throne.

Al-Dhahabī, al-ʿUluww, entry 585, p. 261

The case for “above” is both textual and linguistic. A person’s intended meaning must be understood before judging a translation that uses “on” or “upon”.

Affirmation without tashbīh

A principle of belief

Tashbīh means likening Allah to His creation. In the Athari account of Ahl al-Sunnah presented here, affirming that Allah is above His creation and above His Throne does not mean that His attributes resemble ours. The meaning is affirmed; the manner is not described.

Nothing is like Him

لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِۦ شَىْءٌ ۖ وَهُوَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْبَصِيرُ

Excerpt · English meaning

Nothing is like Him, and He is the Hearing, the Seeing.

Qur’an 42:11

Al-Awzaʿī and al-Dārimī affirm highness; Mālik’s answer forbids pursuing the “how”. Ibn Taymiyyah likewise affirms highness alongside nearness, and rejects the idea that the created heaven carries or shelters Allah. These explanations bring affirmation and incomparability together.

Read al-Awzaʿī, al-Dārimī, Mālik and Ibn Taymiyyah with their sources.

Within this Athari understanding, denying Allah’s highness conflicts with the affirmed doctrine. The expressions “no scholar denied it” and “Ahl al-Sunnah agreed” still need a stated historical scope: the reported ijmāʿ passages preserve their authors’ actual claims. They are not used here to erase the known differences between theological schools or between scholars over additional terminology.

The four explanations listed by Ibn al-Qayyim

Scholarly terminology

Ibn al-Qayyim lists four expressions in his Nūniyyah. These are reported scholarly explanations of istiwāʾ, not four new Qur’anic names or four descriptions of its physical manner.

عَلَا
ʿAlā Was high; rose above.
اِرْتَفَعَ
Irtafaʿa Rose; was elevated.
صَعِدَ
Ṣaʿida Ascended.
اِسْتَقَرَّ
Istaqarra Became established; settled (an ordinary-language gloss). The noun is istiqrār.

These brief English glosses identify the Arabic words. They do not specify how an attribute applies to Allah. The first three concern highness or rising; istiqrār adds a meaning that scholars have discussed separately.

Ibn al-Qayyim, selected Nūniyyah, printed p. 8, verses 61–64

Ibn ʿUthaymīn accepts the four explanations and distinguishes istiqrār from highness alone. Al-Dhahabī expressly prefers not to use istaqarra; al-Albānī also objects to introducing istiqrār as a divine description. Consequently, this is a list reported by Ibn al-Qayyim, not a claim that every Salafi scholar accepted every term.

Ibn ʿUthaymīn, Nūniyyah lesson 16b: the four expressions Al-Albānī, al-Hudā wa-l-nūr, recording 569, item 2

Al-Dhahabī’s reservation about istaqarra

لَا يُعْجِبُنِي قَوْلُهُ اسْتَقَرَّ، بَلْ أَقُولُ كَمَا قَالَ مَالِكٌ الْإِمَامُ: الِاسْتِوَاءُ مَعْلُومٌ

Excerpt · English meaning

I do not favour his saying “istaqarra”; rather, I say as Imam Mālik said: “Istiwāʾ is known.”

Al-Dhahabī, al-ʿUluww, entry 586, p. 262

This reservation concerns an additional expression. It is not a denial of “above”: al-Dhahabī affirms that wording in the preceding discussion.

The clear wording used in this collection remains istiwāʾ, highness and “above”, without a description of the manner.

Jalasa and qaʿada: why this page avoids “sitting”

A disputed explanation

جَلَسَ (jalasa) and قَعَدَ (qaʿada) are words for sitting. They are not the words of the Qur’anic Throne verses, and they are not among the four expressions listed above.

Some scholars transmitted or discussed sitting terminology; others rejected applying it to Allah. Ibn ʿUthaymīn mentions its attribution to some early scholars in one lesson; in another answer, he treats applying it to Allah cautiously and conditionally. Al-Albānī rejects the sitting report under discussion as unestablished and does not accept the wording as an established divine description. That disagreement must be represented honestly.

Calling them simply “the two weak meanings” confuses two questions: whether a particular narration has a sound chain, and whether an explanatory term is justified. A report’s authenticity must be assessed individually; a word itself does not have a chain of transmission.

For that reason, this page does not translate istiwa as “sat down”. It retains the revealed wording and the well-attested explanations of highness, while leaving the manner to Allah.

Ibn ʿUthaymīn, official website: Open Door meeting 11 Ibn ʿUthaymīn, Nūniyyah lesson 49b Al-Albānī, al-Hudā wa-l-nūr, recording 569, item 2

His knowledge encompasses every private conversation

Qur’an

أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَعْلَمُ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ ۖ مَا يَكُونُ مِن نَّجْوَىٰ ثَلَـٰثَةٍ إِلَّا هُوَ رَابِعُهُمْ وَلَا خَمْسَةٍ إِلَّا هُوَ سَادِسُهُمْ وَلَآ أَدْنَىٰ مِن ذَٰلِكَ وَلَآ أَكْثَرَ إِلَّا هُوَ مَعَهُمْ أَيْنَ مَا كَانُوا۟ ۖ ثُمَّ يُنَبِّئُهُم بِمَا عَمِلُوا۟ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَـٰمَةِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ بِكُلِّ شَىْءٍ عَلِيمٌ

English meaning

Do you not see that Allah knows everything in the heavens and on earth? No three people speak privately without Him being their fourth, nor five without Him being their sixth; neither fewer nor more than that without Him being with them wherever they are. Then He will tell them what they did on the Day of Resurrection. Allah knows every thing.

Qur’an 58:7
Context & attribution

The verse begins and ends with Allah’s knowledge. The reported early explanations in the consensus section understand this general maʿiyyah in light of His all-encompassing knowledge.

They cannot encompass Him in knowledge

Qur’an

يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِهِۦ عِلْمًا

English meaning

He knows what is before them and what is behind them, while they do not encompass Him in knowledge.

Qur’an 20:110
Context & attribution

Knowledge of the revealed words is not a claim to know the unseen “how” of Allah’s attributes.

He is near and answers the caller

Qur’an

وَإِذَا سَأَلَكَ عِبَادِى عَنِّى فَإِنِّى قَرِيبٌ ۖ أُجِيبُ دَعْوَةَ ٱلدَّاعِ إِذَا دَعَانِ ۖ فَلْيَسْتَجِيبُوا۟ لِى وَلْيُؤْمِنُوا۟ بِى لَعَلَّهُمْ يَرْشُدُونَ

English meaning

When My servants ask you about Me, I am near. I answer the call of the one who calls upon Me. So let them respond to Me and believe in Me, so that they may be rightly guided.

Qur’an 2:186
Context & attribution

His highness does not mean absence, ignorance of His servants, or inability to hear them. His nearness and response are also affirmed as He has revealed.

None is comparable to Him

Qur’an

وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ

English meaning

And no one is comparable to Him.

Qur’an 112:4
Context & attribution

The Creator is not measured by the properties of His creation.

A note on reading and sources

This collection presents the Athari / Salafi understanding: Allah is above His creation and rose over His Throne in a manner befitting His majesty. He is not contained by creation, nothing resembles Him, and His knowledge encompasses everything.

Qur’anic Arabic is reproduced from the Quran.com text. English passages are translations of meaning prepared for this collection. Excerpts and omissions are marked; the linked sources preserve the fuller context. Hadiths are from the two Sahih collections. Zaynab’s statement is identified as a Companion report.

Reports of ijmāʿ are attributed historical claims with a stated scope. They are not presented as proof that every later Muslim theological school shares the same interpretation. Scholarly words are distinguished from revelation, and reported wording is not silently upgraded into a Prophetic saying.

This is a substantial selection of the principal evidence, not a claim to reproduce every narration or every scholarly discussion. The Arabic original and its full context take precedence over any English rendering here.
